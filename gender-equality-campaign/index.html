The issue: earlier this year, the Politics class conducted a gender equality survey at Hawker College which showed a significant gender gap.

How frequently do you experience sexism? (2)
Male 1.97 / 5
Non-binary 2.75 / 5
Female 3.19 / 5

When asked to rank how frequently they experienced sexism or gender-based discrimination on a Likert scale, with 1 being “never” and 5 being “very often”, male respondents had an average of 1.97, compared to female respondents who had an average of 3.19.

Additionally, when asked if they had ever felt unsafe or judged because of their gender, only 11.4% of males responded “Yes”, compared to a whopping 37.6% of female respondents.

It is impossible to solve the entire gender discrimination problem. and so this campaign will only be focused on curbing the prevalence of misogyny and casualised sexism in schools, targeting the influence of the manosphere and harmful masculinity as root causes.

Campaign Objective

To reduce the frequency of sexism and gender-based discrimination to below 2.5 by 2030.

Primary Targets:

  • Young men at Hawker (cultural)
  • Lyndall Henman (policy)

Secondary Targets:
  • Peer groups of young men (cultural)
  • Student leadership group (policy)

Theory of Change

IF we (Hawker College) implement programs and clubs that help to redefine masculinity, THEN female respondents will feel safer in the school BECAUSE young men will be less likely to perform harmful masculinity.

Monash University will be a crucial contact to have, as they are currently doing a large amount of work on this subject, most notably with their “Introductory Guide to the Manosphere” handbook.

Two other external programs that could be useful to contact are the UK-based #Men4Change toolkit, and the Manhood 2.0 intervention. Both are programs designed to target harmful gender sterotypes and misogynistic behaviour, and improve gender equality outcomes, however we must ensure that this is not pushing content onto the students, but rather serving as a resource to pull them in.
