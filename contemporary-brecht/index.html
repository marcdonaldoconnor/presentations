The Brecht worldview

applications in contemporary theatre

  • As covered by David Barnett in his book, Brecht in Practise, there is no one “Brecht method”. Rather, it is a flexible worldview and set of ideals that can be interpreted and applied to each piece. (Barnett, 2020, p. 83-85)
  • Contemporary theatre does not completely remove empathy from a performance, contrary to Brecht’s desire, but does use Brechtian techniques to help convey deep political and social meaning, ultimately achieving Brecht’s desired goal even if the means may be different.
  • No two pieces of Brecht theatre are the same, or even use the same techniques. For example, The Visitors by Jane Harrison and Fangirls by Yve Blake are two very different plays that both incorporate different Brechtian techniques to communicate socio political meaning.

Brecht’s

Socio-Political Context:

1. The Visitors

2. Fangirls

  • The Visitors is critiquing a system of discrimination against Indigenous people, especially by the government, the systematic erasure of culture, and destruction of being.

  • Fangirls critiques a social culture of patriarchy when dismissing young women.

  • Both plays serve to critique an institutionalised social and political structure.

  • Immediately there are some Brecht techniques at play to simply set their political context.

  • The Visitors uses Brecht’s techniques of Historicization (Barnett, 2020, p. 74) to set the events in a time before white settlers destroyed Indigenous norms.

  • Firstly, this “counter[s] the process of naturalization” (Barnett, 2020, p. 74) to demonstrate to the audience an alternative way of living compared to the modern white capitalist world, which can be used to compare and analyse our modern society.

  • Secondly, by not revealing the exact time and date to the audience straight away, it gives the feeling of a “slow dawning” on the audience as they realise the inevitability of what is about to happen.

Irony in particular serves an important literary device in The Visitors, as modern audiences understand the harmful consequences of white settlement despite Gordon’s inclusive, welcoming attitude at the end of the play. This is a particularly Brechtian technique, to let the audience be aware of the conclusion before telling the story, as it furthers a disconnect from empathy and emotion.

As The Visitors reveals the imperfect social dynamics between Indigenous nations throughout, irony is used to ensure that – even though the Indigenous elders suffer from the same human errors as the rest of us – the audience understands the action taken against them by white settlers was unjust.

On Fangirls:

  • One-liners, especially about taboo subjects or sensitive issues, is both unexpected and adds humour to the performance (e.g. “MY UTERUS IS EXPLODING!” (Blake, 2024, p. 26))
  • “The striking nature of these approaches confronts spectators with surprising or unexpected representations, thus distancing them from the action on stage and disrupting empathy.” (Barnett, 2020, p. 107)
  • This is the Verfremdungseffekt at play; making the familiar strange and forcing audiences to judge, and therefore critically analyse what they are seeing on stage.

Gestus

  • The Visitors intentionally uses character archetypes, and gestus helps to reinforce those developmental boundaries.
  • Walter’s repetitive gazing, leaning inwards, and silent movement all reflect his preference for wisdom, thought, and cultural power within the group.
  • Lawrence’s repeated sneezing and sickness demonstrates how, being the youngest and least experienced in the group, he is the first to fall victim to colonial rule.
  • The “message stick” as a cultural ritual is a group gestus (?) (culture as gestus?)

Set of ‘Fangirls’ (“‘Fangirls’ Review,” 2024)

Fangirls opts for a very dynamic and fluid sense of staging, quickly shifting from one location to the next or even having multiple locations on the stage at the same time. Combined with screens and digital projections behind the actors, the stage has a minimal set that can be reconfigured extremely quickly, a feature consistent with Brecht’s epic theatre (Cash, 2022).

  • Whilst The Visitors is only set in one location, a rocky outcrop overlooking Botany Bay, it still features a similarly simple set as in epic theatre, with the back walls of the stage simply being black curtains in most productions.
  • The height of the rock is a major blocking feature, used to represent power within the group.

Brecht says “from what happens between them [characters], people get everything that can be discussed, criticised, changed.” (Rouse, 1984, p. 28) - This is why The Visitors is effective as a Brechtian piece of theatre, as the entire play is set as a debate between characters, exactly as Brecht would have wanted.

Theoretically, The Visitors could be a great play to consider staging in the round (debate/discussion plays are great for this), but Jane Harrison chose not to because she wanted to treat the audience as one foreign entity intruding, not surrounding.

Actor-Audience

Relationship

  • “[Jane Harrison] does this not by alienating the audience but by drawing them in through familiar constructions of debate and discussion…” (Harrison, 2021, p. x)

  • The Visitors doesn’t talk directly to the audience, rather it treats the audience as the “look out to sea” towards the foreign British ships, therefore discussing and evaluating the audience (and the ideals of British society) in front of them. This feeling of being watched and judged makes the audience feel uncomfortable, alienated, and like they shouldn’t be there (almost like they are Visitors……. I’ll see myself out). Harrison does alienate the audience, but in a good way.

  • Conversly, Fangirls does speak directly to the audience, treating theatre-goers as in-universe for concerts and as part of the fan group.

  • The script actually calls for two actors that go out during the end of the interval to interact with the crowd and rile everybody up into a chant (Blake, 2024, p. 43).

  • Engaging the audience helps them notice how this is fiction

The concert is an integral part of Fangirls, as being a musical allows Fangirls to explore social dynamics in a non-threatening, non-confronting way. > transition

The Elders gather around a rock (2023)

  • The Visitors uses costuming to represent the socio-political status of the elders; connecting them to modern-day social heirarchies (suits)
  • Establishing the elders as voices of authority and power is critical for a world that stereotypes and minimises Indigenous people as at the bottom of the social heirarchy.

Conclusion

  • The Visitors employs more empathy, demonstrating how Brecht has mixed with traditional emotional response to produce contemporary theatre.
  • Fangirls, on the other hand, employs more Spass and absurdism to create a comedy.
  • Both are unique approaches to the application of Brecht in a contemporary setting, and serve to demonstrate how Brechtian theatre has become synonomous with modern contemporary theatre.
